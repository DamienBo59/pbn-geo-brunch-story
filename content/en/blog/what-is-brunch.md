> **In short:**
> 1. Brunch is a single meal served between **11 am and 3 pm** that replaces both breakfast and lunch.
> 2. The word dates from **1895**: it was proposed by Guy Beringer in a London piece titled *Brunch: A Plea*.
> 3. What separates it from a late breakfast is the **compulsory savoury element**, almost always egg-based.
> 4. Budget **6 to 10 euros per person** at home, against **20 to 35 euros** at a city-centre venue.

## The definition, in one sentence

**Brunch** is a single meal, eaten late in the morning, that replaces breakfast and lunch. The word is a contraction of *breakfast* and *lunch*, and it says exactly what it means.

The idea is old and documented. In 1895 the Englishman **Guy Beringer** published a short piece titled *Brunch: A Plea*, suggesting that the heavy Sunday post-church meal be replaced with something lighter, served around noon, with family or friends. The habit took hold in the United States in the 1930s, and became a weekend ritual in French cities from the 2000s onwards.

### What is not brunch

The most common confusion is with a late breakfast. The difference rests on two simple criteria:

- **There is savoury food.** A breakfast served at noon is still a breakfast. With no eggs, no cheese, nothing savoury, it is not brunch.
- **It lasts.** Brunch runs **two to three hours**, served continuously, where breakfast takes twenty minutes.

## The real timing, in venues and at home

In most places service starts between **11 am and noon** and ends around **3 pm**. Sunday carries the bulk of the activity, with Saturday far behind.

At home the optimal slot is **11:30 am**. Earlier, guests have already had coffee and something to nibble, and the appetite is not there. Later, the meal spills into the afternoon and dinner becomes an awkward question.

## What goes on the table

This is where most home brunches spiral into fifteen preparations and a wrecked kitchen. Four families are enough for a complete table.

| Family | Examples | Per person |
|---|---|---|
| Base | bread, bagel, pastry, toast | 2 items |
| Savoury | eggs, avocado toast, salmon, cheese, ham | 3 to 4 items |
| Sweet | pancakes, fruit salad, granola, jam | 2 items |
| Drinks | coffee, juice, water, tea | 2 cups + 200 ml juice |

### The rule that simplifies everything

**Only one preparation is made to order, everything else waits.** In practice that means eggs are the only thing you cook while guests are there. All the rest, pancakes included, is made ahead or served cold.

Cooking times are covered in [the poached egg timing guide](/en/blog/poached-egg-timing/), the most technical item on the table and the only one that punishes guesswork.

## The budget, and what you actually pay for in a restaurant

A city-centre brunch costs **20 to 35 euros** per person, often as an all-you-can-eat formula. The same content, made at home, comes to **6 to 10 euros**.

The gap is not evenly spread. Three items carry most of the margin:

1. **Pastries**, bought at 1 to 1.20 euro each and counted for considerably more in the formula.
2. **Smoked salmon**, the most expensive item on the table, usually served in 30 to 40 g portions.
3. **Coffee**, whose raw cost is around **50 cents a cup** and which is almost always the weak point of the meal. The subject is developed in [the brunch coffee guide](/en/blog/brunch-coffee/).

What you legitimately pay for is not doing the dishes and not cooking while everyone else eats. That is a real service and it has a price.

## Adapting the table to your guests

Brunch gathers six to eight people, so statistically at least one dietary constraint. Two cover most cases.

- **Gluten free**: the bread and pastry base disappears entirely, which empties the table if nothing is planned. Concrete options are in [the gluten-free breakfast guide](/en/blog/gluten-free-breakfast/).
- **Vegetarian**: the savoury side then rests on eggs, cheese and avocado, which needs no extra preparation.

<summary>What should you put on the table for brunch?</summary>

<details>
<summary>How much does a homemade brunch cost?</summary>

Between 6 and 10 euros per person for a complete homemade brunch, against 20 to 35 euros at a city-centre venue. The gap comes mostly from pastries, smoked salmon and coffee, the three items carrying the highest margin in restaurants.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the difference between brunch and breakfast?</summary>

Breakfast is sweet, short and eaten early in the morning. Brunch always includes savoury food, is eaten between 11 am and 3 pm, and replaces two meals instead of one. A breakfast served late is still a breakfast.

</details>
