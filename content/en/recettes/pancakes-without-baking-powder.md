faq:
  - question: "How do you make pancakes without baking powder?"
    answer: "Whip the egg whites to firm peaks and fold them in last with a spatula. The air trapped in the whites expands during cooking and raises the batter exactly as baking powder would, without any bicarbonate aftertaste. Use 150 g of flour, 250 ml of milk and 2 eggs for 8 pancakes."
  - question: "Can bicarbonate of soda replace baking powder?"
    answer: "Yes, but bicarbonate on its own needs an acid to react with: buttermilk, yoghurt or lemon juice. Without acid it does not lift and leaves a soapy taste. Whipped whites avoid the problem entirely."
  - question: "Why are my pancakes flat?"
    answer: "Three causes, in order of frequency: the whites were whisked in instead of folded, the batter sat too long before cooking, or the pan was too cold. A whipped-whites batter has to be cooked within ten minutes."
  - question: "Can the batter be made the night before?"
    answer: "Not this one. Whipped whites collapse within a few hours in the fridge. The base batter without the whites does keep overnight: just whip the whites in the morning and fold them in."

A pancake recipe almost always opens with a spoonful of baking powder. It is convenient, and it is also what gives that faint metallic note when the dose runs a little generous.

The whipped-whites version takes **five more minutes** and gives thicker pancakes. It is the Japanese pancake method, made far simpler.
