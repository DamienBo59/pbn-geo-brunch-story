faq:
  - question: "How do you make proper hot chocolate at home?"
    answer: "With melted dark chocolate, not cocoa powder. Use 40 g of 70 % chocolate per 200 ml of whole milk per cup. Heat the milk to 75 or 80 °C without boiling, take it off the heat, add the chopped chocolate and a pinch of salt, then whisk. Rest it 5 to 10 minutes off the heat and it thickens on its own."
  - question: "Why is my hot chocolate too thin?"
    answer: "Because it was made with cocoa powder, which is defatted and holds only 10 to 12 % fat, against roughly 40 % for 70 % dark chocolate. The cocoa butter is what makes it coat the spoon, not the amount of powder."
  - question: "Can it be made with a plant-based milk?"
    answer: "Yes, oat gives the best result because it is naturally sweet and thick. Almond milk makes it thinner: in that case go up to 50 g of chocolate per 200 ml."
  - question: "Can it be made ahead for brunch?"
    answer: "Yes. It keeps 48 hours in the fridge and reheats gently in a pan, without boiling. It will have thickened in the meantime, so just add a splash of milk and whisk."

Hot chocolate at a brunch spot often costs 6 euros and arrives lukewarm, oversweet, with a powdery taste. This one costs around **90 cents a cup** and takes ten minutes.

Only one rule matters: **chocolate, not cocoa**. Everything else follows from it.
